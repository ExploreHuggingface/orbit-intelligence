Berlin residential is a speed game wearing a patience costume. The listings worth seeing often move in days, and the
ones that linger are priced accordingly. Here is how to set up ORBIT for a yield-first hunt — and how to read what
it shows you.

## 1. Describe the hunt, not the database query

> Apartments in Berlin-Friedrichshain and nearby districts under €350k, vacant or expiring tenancy preferred,
> estimated gross yield above 5.5%.

Plain language works. ORBIT derives the structured criteria — category, price band, location, yield floor — and you
refine them in the criteria step: add *Kreuzberg* and *Neukölln*, exclude *WBS* (income-restricted) units, set
sensitivity to **Balanced**.

## 2. Read matches the yield-first way

Each match arrives with the deal analysis computed:

- **Asking price vs reference band** — the market context in one line
- **Price per m²** — the Berlin number that actually trends
- **Est. rent and gross yield** — from comparable rentals in the neighborhood
- **Net yield** — after an 18% expense load for tax, maintenance and management

A probate sale in Neukölln listing 7.8% below band with a 4.4% gross yield is a very different animal from a
renovated Prenzlauer Berg condo at reference with 5.6% — and ORBIT surfaces that difference before you book a
viewing.

## 3. Compare, then move

Drop the three finalists into a comparison: price per m², rent estimate, gross and net yields line up side by side,
and the best value per row gets a quiet highlight. From there it's the source listing, the expose PDF, and the
email — you, but faster.

## A note on the numbers

Rents are estimated from comparable listings; yields are gross of financing and transaction costs; all figures are
illustrative estimates, not appraisals. The assumptions are one click away on every property record — read them
before you act on anything.
